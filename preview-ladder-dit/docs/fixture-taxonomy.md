# Synthetic fixture taxonomy

Preview Ladder DiT fixtures are deterministic stress tests for preview-final consistency in masked video replacement. They are not evidence that a real video diffusion backend works. They are a public contract for which failure axes the benchmark can detect before LTX-backed experiments are available.

Each fixture is emitted with `metadata.failure_axis`, `metadata.expected_detector_metrics`, `metadata.generation_seed`, `metadata.difficulty`, `metadata.failure_description`, and calibrated `metadata.expected_metric_bands`. Benchmark manifests copy the same taxonomy fields into every task so report consumers do not need to parse fixture internals.

| Case | Axis | Difficulty | Primary detectors | Production analogue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `clean` | control | control | `preview_final_l1` | Accepted preview is preserved exactly. This is the sanity check for report, scorecard, and aggregation code. |
| `identity_drift` | appearance | easy | `preview_final_l1`, `low_frequency_drift` | Replacement color or identity shifts between preview approval and final render. |
| `boundary_halo` | boundary | easy | `boundary_consistency_error`, `boundary_signed_bias` | The final render adds a visible seam, matte fringe, or bleed near the mask boundary. |
| `background_leak` | background | easy | `background_preservation_error`, `background_temporal_leak` | Locked source pixels change because inpainting influences areas outside the edit mask. |
| `motion_mismatch` | motion | medium | `trajectory_center_error`, `occupancy_iou_error` | Final object trajectory differs from the approved preview even if average appearance remains plausible. |
| `temporal_flicker` | temporal | easy | `temporal_flicker_delta`, `local_temporal_residual_flicker` | Final replacement flickers or pulses despite a stable accepted preview. |
| `occlusion_failure` | occlusion | medium | `mask_occupancy_delta`, `occupancy_iou_error` | The inserted object disappears under partial occlusion or loses occupancy in later frames. |
| `mask_instability` | mask | medium | `temporal_edge_jitter`, `mask_occupancy_delta` | Effective edit support jitters frame to frame, producing unstable silhouettes. |
| `thin_structure_boundary` | thin_structure | hard | `boundary_consistency_error`, `occupancy_iou_error` | Hair, cables, tools, or narrow limbs approved in preview are dropped or softened in final. |
| `illumination_pulse` | illumination | medium | `temporal_flicker_delta`, `low_frequency_drift` | Final introduces a coherent brightness pulse that was not in the preview. |
| `shadow_leak` | background | medium | `background_preservation_error`, `boundary_consistency_error` | Replacement casts an unapproved shadow into locked background pixels. |
| `parallax_mismatch` | camera_motion | hard | `trajectory_center_error`, `temporal_flicker_delta` | Preview and final disagree under camera motion or parallax, so the object slides against the scene. |

## Calibration rule

Expected metric bands are regression bands, not leaderboard thresholds. A fixture should pass its own band when generated by the deterministic harness. A submitted method can still fail product scorecard gates even if it lands inside a fixture band, because the scorecard aggregates boundary, background, motion, appearance, temporal, occupancy, and commitment preservation risk.

## Why this matters

The taxonomy keeps the benchmark hard to game in three ways:

1. Every task declares its intended failure axis and detector metrics in the manifest.
2. Reports include schema identity, artifact hashes, and latency milestones, so fixture scores can be tied back to a method and generated artifacts.
3. Synthetic evidence is explicitly scoped. It proves that the report, metric, scorecard, policy, and CLI path can detect controlled failures. It does not prove real LTX quality until GPU-backed preview/final runs are added.
